[Distributed transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_transaction) change data on more than one database node. [Atomic commit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_commit) gives all participants one outcome: commit or abort. [Two-Phase Commit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-phase_commit_protocol) (2PC) is a common way to reach that outcome.

## Two-Phase Commit

A coordinator manages the transaction. The database nodes taking part in the transaction are called participants. First, the coordinator asks whether each participant can commit. Then, based on how the participants vote, it sends everyone the same decision (either commit or abort).

### Phase One: Prepare

The coordinator sends **PREPARE** to every participant, asking whether it can commit.

After PREPARE arrives each participant checks whether it can commit. A yes voter saves its prepared state and sends **YES**. A no voter rolls back its local transaction, releases its locks, and sends **NO**.

### Phase Two: Commit or Abort

If everyone votes yes, the coordinator chooses **COMMIT**. If anyone votes no, it chooses **ABORT**. It can also abort if a vote does not arrive in time, as long as it has not already recorded commit. It records the decision in storage that survives a restart before sending it.

Each participant saves the outcome so it survives a restart, then commits its changes or rolls back its local transaction. It releases its locks and sends an acknowledgement.

## Guarantees and Costs

2PC gives participants one commit-or-abort outcome. The extra coordination has a cost:

- Votes and decisions take network round trips.
- Participants and the coordinator must save records for recovery.
- Slow participants delay the transaction.
- Prepared participants may hold locks while they wait.

## Failures and Recovery

A participant that voted yes cannot safely abort just because it times out. The coordinator may already have recorded commit and told another participant. If the decision is unavailable, the prepared participant can remain [blocked](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~bestchai/teaching/cs416_2016w2/lectures/lecture-mar22-part2.pdf#page=2), holding locks while it waits.

## Distributed SQL

Distributed SQL databases combine transaction coordination across shards with consensus replication within each shard. Systems such as [Spanner](https://cloud.google.com/spanner), [TiDB](https://www.pingcap.com/tidb/), [YugabyteDB](https://www.yugabyte.com/), and [CockroachDB](https://www.cockroachlabs.com/product/cockroachdb/) use these building blocks to keep transactions consistent across replicated data.
