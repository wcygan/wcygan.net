A $10 transfer changes one account by <span className="transaction-amount transaction-amount--debit">−$10</span> and another by <span className="transaction-amount transaction-amount--credit">+$10</span>. Atomic commitment makes both changes succeed or fail together.

Here, “distributed” means the accounts live on separate database shards. A transaction local to one shard cannot protect changes on both. [MySQL XA transactions](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/9.7/en/xa.html) use two-phase commit (2PC) to coordinate multiple transactional resources.

## Two-Phase Commit

Two-Phase Commit (2PC) uses a coordinator to agree on one outcome across participating shards. In MySQL XA, a client transaction manager fills that role. At a high level, it follows this process:

1. **Prepare:** The coordinator asks every shard to <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--prepare">PREPARE</span> and waits for each vote (yes or no).
2. **Commit or abort:** If all vote yes, the coordinator records and sends <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--commit">COMMIT</span>; otherwise, it records and sends <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--abort">ABORT</span>. Each shard applies the decision, records the outcome, and acknowledges it.

After every shard votes yes, an unavailable coordinator can leave prepared shards [blocked](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~bestchai/teaching/cs416_2016w2/lectures/lecture-mar22-part2.pdf#page=2). They cannot safely choose <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--commit">commit</span> or <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--abort">abort</span> from a timeout alone. If the coordinator stays unavailable, the wait has no bound. When it recovers, it checks its durable transaction log and resends any recorded decision; the shards keep their locks until they receive and apply it.

In this account-row example, a prepared transaction holds locks on the [rows it touched](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/9.7/en/innodb-locking.html). Work on unrelated rows can continue, while [writes that need those locked rows must wait](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/9.7/en/innodb-information-schema-understanding-innodb-locking.html) for the decision.

## Three-Phase Commit

Three-Phase Commit (3PC) adds a pre-commit round before the final decision to reduce blocking when the coordinator fails.

With bounded message and processing delays and no network partition, 3PC can avoid the coordinator-failure blocking problem of 2PC. Outside those assumptions, a timeout cannot reliably distinguish a crashed participant from an unreachable one.

During a network partition, a participant that received <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--pre-commit">PRE-COMMIT</span> may time out and <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--abort"><a href="https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~bestchai/teaching/cs416_2016w2/lectures/lecture-mar22-part2.pdf#page=10">commit while participants that did not receive it abort</a></span>, violating atomicity. The extra phase allows progress only while the timing and connectivity assumptions hold.

## Transactions Across Replicated Shards

Sharding spreads data across groups. Replication stores multiple copies of each group's data. A transaction that spans replicated shards needs one consistent outcome across the groups.

Production distributed databases combine these effects in different ways:

- [Google Spanner](<https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/whitepapers/life-of-reads-and-writes#:~:text=We%20implement%20two%2Dphase%20commit%20(2PC)%20for%20transactions%20that%20span%20splits>) replicates each split with Paxos and uses two-phase commit for transactions that span splits. TrueTime supports its commit-wait step.
- In [TiDB and TiKV](https://docs.pingcap.com/tidb/stable/tikv-overview/), each TiKV Region is replicated with Raft. In the standard 2PC path, TiDB's TiKV client [prewrites mutations](https://github.com/tikv/client-go/blob/b9fc0b7719d3ea62bd9904cd31fbd27715ff08bc/txnkv/transaction/2pc.go#L1895-L1898) and then [commits them](https://github.com/tikv/client-go/blob/b9fc0b7719d3ea62bd9904cd31fbd27715ff08bc/txnkv/transaction/2pc.go#L2070-L2079); TiKV's storage code [writes the prepared lock](https://github.com/tikv/tikv/blob/548812e1ef57aef077a2062a9cc356640a6347f5/src/storage/txn/actions/prewrite.rs#L750-L767) and [records the commit](https://github.com/tikv/tikv/blob/548812e1ef57aef077a2062a9cc356640a6347f5/src/storage/txn/actions/commit.rs#L218-L244).
- [CockroachDB](https://www.cockroachlabs.com/blog/parallel-commits/) replicates key ranges with Raft and uses its [Parallel Commits protocol](https://github.com/cockroachdb/cockroach/blob/d30c905fff79ef825adc96bcc647f1872a90f2ff/pkg/kv/kvclient/kvcoord/txn_interceptor_committer.go#L166-L259), an atomic commit optimization for transactions across ranges.

The demo transfers $10 from Shard A to Shard B. Each shard has three replicas, and two replicas form a **quorum**: enough copies to safely record a decision. A's leader also coordinates the transfer.

1. **Lock both accounts.** A and B hold their account locks while the transfer is pending.
2. **B prepares and tells A it is ready.** B stores <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--prepare">PREPARE</span> on two replicas before sending its Yes vote to A.
3. **A records the decision.** A stores <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--commit">COMMIT</span> on two replicas, together with its debit and the commit timestamp. The decision is now fixed.
4. **Both shards apply the transfer.** After a short clock-related wait, A applies the debit and sends <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--commit">COMMIT</span> to B. B records it on two replicas, applies the credit, and releases its lock. The balances finish at $90 and $110.

This is a simplified version of [Spanner’s protocol (§4.2.1)](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/osdi12/osdi12-final-16.pdf#page=8): A skips its own <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--prepare">PREPARE</span> record, and the clock-related wait is specific to Spanner. Replicas with blank records can catch up later. The displayed balances show internal progress; a consistent read cannot return the temporary $90/$100 pair.
