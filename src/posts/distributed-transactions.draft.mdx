[Atomic Commitment algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_commit) are used to make multiple operations appear like they are executed atomically. For example, transferring $10 between accounts should appear like <span className="transaction-amount transaction-amount--credit">+$10</span> and <span className="transaction-amount transaction-amount--debit">−$10</span> against different accounts in one database transaction instead of two. To make sure balance is not lost, either both of these operations should occur, or neither.

"Distributed" here means that the database is sharded. Since accounts may live on separate shards, single-node [Transcations](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/9.7/en/glossary.html#glos_transaction) will not suffice, and we need to reach for [Distributed Transactions](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/9.7/en/glossary.html#glos_transaction:~:text=Also%20cursor.-,two%2Dphase%20commit,-An%20operation%20that) to ensure the changes are applied consistently across shards.

MySQL uses [Two-Phase Commit](<https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/9.7/en/xa.html#:~:text=The%20process%20for%20executing%20a%20global%20transaction%20uses%20two%2Dphase%20commit%20(2PC)>) to support distributed transactions.

## Two-Phase Commit

Two-Phase Commit (2PC) uses a coordinator to orchestrate progress across the cluster. At a high level, it follows this process:

1. **Prepare:** The coordinator asks every shard to prepare and waits for each vote (yes or no).
2. **Commit or abort:** If all vote yes, the coordinator records and sends <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--commit">COMMIT</span>; otherwise, it records and sends <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--abort">ABORT</span>. Each shard applies the decision, records the outcome, and acknowledges it.

In 2PC, the <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--abort"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-phase_commit_protocol#Disadvantages:~:text=The%20greatest%20disadvantage%20of%20the%20two%2Dphase%20commit%20protocol%20is%20that%20it%20is%20a%20blocking%20protocol">worst case</a></span> is that the transaction can block indefinitely.

After every shard votes yes, a coordinator failure before its decision reaches them leaves prepared shards unable to safely choose commit or abort. They [block until they receive a commit or rollback decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-phase_commit_protocol#Disadvantages:~:text=If%20the%20coordinator%20fails%20permanently%2C%20some%20participants%20will%20never%20resolve%20their%20transactions%3A%20After%20a%20participant%20has%20sent%20an%20agreement%20message%20as%20a%20response%20to%20the%20commit%2Drequest%20message%20from%20the%20coordinator%2C%20it%20will%20block%20until%20a%20commit%20or%20rollback%20is%20received.). When the coordinator restarts, it checks its durable transaction log and resends any recorded decision; the shards keep their locks until they receive and apply it.

This does not stop a shard from doing all other work: the prepared transaction holds locks on the [rows it touched](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/9.7/en/innodb-locking.html#:~:text=InnoDB%20implements%20standard%20row%2Dlevel%20locking). Work on unrelated rows can continue, while [writes that need those locked rows must wait](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/9.7/en/innodb-information-schema-understanding-innodb-locking.html#:~:text=The%20requesting%20transaction%20must%20wait%20for%20the%20transaction%20that%20holds%20the%20blocking%20lock%20to%20commit%20or%20roll%20back.%20If%20a%20transaction%20is%20not%20waiting%20for%20a%20lock%2C%20it%20is%20in%20a%20RUNNING%20state.%20If%20a%20transaction%20is%20waiting%20for%20a%20lock%2C%20it%20is%20in%20a%20LOCK%20WAIT%20state.) for the decision.

## Three-Phase Commit

Three-Phase Commit (3PC) adds a pre-commit round before the final decision to reduce blocking when the coordinator fails.

In 3PC, the <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--abort"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-phase_commit_protocol#Disadvantages:~:text=Three%2Dphase%20commit%20assumes%20a%20network%20with%20bounded%20delay%20and%20nodes%20with%20bounded%20response%20times">worst case</a></span> is that a network partition can leave participants with different outcomes.

3PC relies on bounded message and processing delays. If a partition prevents some shards from receiving <span className="transaction-decision transaction-decision--pre-commit">PRE-COMMIT</span>, participants that received it may time out and commit while others time out and abort, violating atomicity. The extra phase reduces blocking only while those timing assumptions hold.
